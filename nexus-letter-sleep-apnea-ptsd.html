Nexus Letters for Sleep Apnea Secondary to PTSD

Written by Tim · Founder, Waypoint Veteran Group · Updated 2026

If you're service-connected for PTSD and have also been diagnosed with obstructive sleep apnea, you may be entitled to an additional rating — but only if the VA can see a clear medical connection between the two conditions. That connection is usually established with a nexus letter. Here's what that means and how the process typically works.

What is a nexus letter?

A nexus letter is a written medical opinion from a qualified provider that addresses whether a condition is, in VA terms, “at least as likely as not” connected to military service or to another condition that's already service-connected. It's not a diagnosis by itself — it's an opinion that explains the connection, grounded in the veteran's medical history and relevant clinical reasoning.

Why sleep apnea and PTSD are often connected

Sleep apnea can be claimed on its own, but it's frequently claimed as secondary to PTSD instead. The reasoning generally centers on a few recognized patterns: PTSD is associated with disrupted sleep architecture, and weight gain — sometimes linked to PTSD-related medication, reduced activity, or sleep disruption itself — is a well-established risk factor for obstructive sleep apnea. A nexus letter addressing this connection typically walks through your specific history rather than relying on the general pattern alone.

What medical evidence typically supports this type of claim

  • A current diagnosis of obstructive sleep apnea, usually from a sleep study
  • An existing service-connected rating for PTSD (or clear evidence supporting one)
  • Documentation of the timeline — when PTSD symptoms and sleep-related symptoms or weight changes began, relative to each other
  • A nexus letter that explains, in medical terms specific to your history, why the connection is at least as likely as not

What a nexus letter does not do

A nexus letter is one piece of evidence in a larger claims file — it doesn't replace a complete record, and it doesn't guarantee any particular rating or outcome. The VA still weighs it alongside your full history. Veterans considering this route are usually better served by first having their existing records reviewed to see whether the timeline and documentation actually support a secondary connection, rather than requesting a letter first and hoping the history fits.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a nexus letter?

A nexus letter is a written medical opinion, from a qualified provider, that addresses whether a current condition is at least as likely as not connected to a veteran's military service or to another already service-connected condition.

Why are sleep apnea and PTSD often connected in VA claims?

PTSD can disrupt sleep and contribute to weight changes, both recognized risk factors for obstructive sleep apnea — which is why sleep apnea is frequently claimed as secondary to service-connected PTSD rather than as a standalone condition.

Does a nexus letter guarantee my claim will be approved?

No. It's one piece of evidence the VA weighs alongside your full claims file, and it doesn't guarantee any specific rating or outcome.
